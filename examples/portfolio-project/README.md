# EXAMPLE: Personal portfolio

> **Fictional worked example.** Not a real project. It shows how grilling changes the brief, and what a G1 rejection looks like.

**Request:** *"Create my personal portfolio."*

---

## S0 — Routing

```
ROUTING BLOCK
Mode:         personal-portfolio  (confidence: High)
Runner-up:    none
Stage:        S0 -> S1
Questions:    5 (batched)
Assumptions:  Index + case studies. No CMS, no backend. Vercel Hobby.
              You write the copy. Content co-located.
Documents:    PROJECT.md, DESIGN.md, ARCHITECTURE.md, TASKS.md, AGENTS.md,
              HANDOFF.md, QA.md, RETROSPECTIVE.md
Skills:       discovery, grilling (mandatory), reference-analysis,
              design-direction, asset-generation, frontend-build, motion-design,
              browser-qa, accessibility, performance, evaluation, deployment
Owner:        Strategist on R1
Gates ahead:  G1, G2, G3, G4
Budget:       none — Vercel Hobby is free for non-commercial
First action: Answer the 5 questions.
```

**Questions:** which 3-5 projects and what each proves · who is the reader · the one thing to be remembered for · process artifacts or only finals · deadline.

---

## S1 — Grilling changes the brief

This is why grilling is mandatory in this mode. The first answers are always the plausible ones.

| Asked | First answer | After pushing |
|---|---|---|
| Who is the reader? | "Anyone hiring designers" | Three specific studios whose work is motion-led |
| What is the goal? | "Show my work" | Get an interview at one of those three |
| Which projects? | "All eight" | **Three.** The other five dilute the motion positioning. |
| What is the one thing? | "That I'm versatile" | That interaction is where the thinking happens |

**"Versatile" to "interaction is where the thinking happens" is the entire value of this stage.** The first version produces a grid of eight thumbnails — a template. The second produces a structural decision: the site must *demonstrate* interaction thinking, not describe it.

**Non-goals that emerged:** no blog · no client-services page · not showing the five weaker projects even though they took real effort.

---

## S3 — Direction, and a rejected first attempt

**First attempt at a thesis** — *"A clean, modern portfolio with smooth animations that feels premium and showcases my work."*

**Rejected at G1.** It fails the test in [design-direction](../../skills/design-direction.md): it does not tell you what to do when someone asks for a hero image. Every banned adjective is present. A generic template would satisfy it.

**Second attempt** — *"Each case study is a working interaction, not a description of one. The portfolio is the demo."*

That is a thesis. It rules things out immediately: no static screenshot galleries, no video walkthroughs of interactions that could be live, no "process" sections that describe motion in prose.

**Scorecard at G1:** 44/50 — lowest criterion *Material quality* at 3, because the treatment was undecided. Flagged as the thing to resolve during S4 rather than left implicit.

**Signature moment:** the index is a single continuous interaction; scrubbing through it *is* navigating the case studies. Mobile equivalent: drag rather than scroll-scrub.

---

## The trap this example is about

**Showing everything.** Eight projects felt like proof of range. It reads as an inability to judge your own work — and the weakest of the eight sets the perceived level for a reviewer who has 60 seconds and a hundred other portfolios.

Three projects, each proving one thing, is a stronger position and a shorter build.

---

## S5 — Review

Lenses: creative director, portfolio reviewer, design-school reviewer. Run in **fresh sessions**, given only the deployed URL and the success criteria.

The design-school lens is the one to actually run. It is entitled to say the work is competent and pointless, and here it asked the uncomfortable question directly: *is the site better than the work in it?* On a portfolio whose thesis is "the portfolio is the demo", that question is load-bearing rather than rhetorical.

---

## What this example demonstrates

| Point | Where |
|---|---|
| Grilling changes the actual brief | "Versatile" to a specific positioning |
| Curation beats volume | Eight projects to three |
| G1 can and should reject a weak thesis | The first attempt, verbatim |
| A thesis rules things out | No screenshot galleries, no video walkthroughs |
| An honest scorecard names its weakest criterion | 44/50 with material quality at 3 |
| Reviews run in fresh sessions | URL and criteria only |
