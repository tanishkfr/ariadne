# MODE: Personal portfolio

Your own work, your own reputation. **The highest creative ceiling and the least external constraint** — which is exactly what makes it hard.

There is no client to say no, so nothing stops a portfolio from becoming a competent, forgettable template except the rules in this system.

---

## Detection

**Signals:** "my portfolio", "my site", "about me", "my work", "showcase".

**Not this mode if:** it is for someone else (premium client website), or the portfolio *is* an interactive product (product app, with portfolio as a secondary lens).

---

## Questions

1. **Which 3-5 projects, and what should each one prove?** — no default; this is the whole brief
2. **Who is the reader — recruiter, studio, client, or awards jury?** — default: studio and serious client
3. **What is the one thing you want remembered?** — no default
4. **Do you have process artifacts, or only finished screens?** — default: assume finals only, and design around that
5. **Is there a deadline — an application, a submission?** — default: none

Question 1 does the most work. "All of my projects" is not an answer; **the weakest piece sets the perceived level.**

## Documents

`PROJECT.md` → `DESIGN.md` → `ARCHITECTURE.md` → `TASKS.md` → `AGENTS.md` → `HANDOFF.md` → `QA.md` → `RETROSPECTIVE.md`

S2 research is **light** — reference analysis matters, live research usually does not.

## Skills

discovery, **grilling (mandatory)**, reference-analysis, design-direction, asset-generation, frontend-build, motion-design, browser-qa, accessibility, performance, evaluation, deployment

## Gates

G1, G3, G4. G2 as needed. G5 does not apply.

**G1 matters most here**, because there is no client to reject a weak direction. You are the only check, and you are the one who wants to start building.

---

## Quality bar

- Scorecard **43+**
- Lenses: creative director, **portfolio reviewer**, **design-school reviewer**
- Portfolio reviewer below 32 is not competitive
- The **recall test** is the real bar: *would someone remember this tomorrow, among a hundred others?*

The design-school lens is the uncomfortable one and the one to actually run. It is entitled to say the work is competent and pointless, and that verdict is the most useful output available.

---

## The specific traps

**Showing everything.** Four strong pieces beat eight mixed ones. The weakest piece sets the perceived level, and a reviewer with 60 seconds will find it.

**Case studies that describe features.** "I built a filter system" is a feature. "The filter had to work before the data loaded, so I inverted the fetch order" is a decision. Reviewers hire for decisions.

**No process visible.** Finished screens prove taste. Process proves thinking, which is what studios are actually assessing. If you do not have process artifacts, say so and design around it — do not fake them.

**Designing for yourself instead of the reader.** A portfolio is a persuasion document with an audience and a goal, not a personal expression. The grilling skill exists to make you say what opportunity this is for.

**The signature moment being the whole site.** One memorable device, executed everywhere, beats five clever moments. See [DESIGN-TASTE.md](../DESIGN-TASTE.md) 1.2.

---

## Failure modes

| Failure | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Endless polish, never shipped | Set a real deadline at S1, even an artificial one |
| Including weak work to look prolific | Question 1; curate ruthlessly |
| No direction, because no client said no | G1 with an honest scorecard |
| Case studies as feature lists | Write decisions, and what was rejected |
| Rebuilding it every six months instead of using it | Ship, then evaluate against the actual goal |
| The site is better than the work in it | An uncomfortable but real finding — the design-school lens will say it |
