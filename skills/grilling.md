# SKILL: grilling

**Trigger** — `PROJECT.md` is drafted and the scope still feels soft; or you ask to be stress-tested.
**Owner** — Strategist · **Class** — `R1`
**Inputs** — draft `PROJECT.md`
**Output** — revised `PROJECT.md`, contradictions resolved or logged

Mandatory for premium client website, personal portfolio, product app, content system. Skipped for game/experiment and benchmark, where being wrong costs an afternoon rather than a reputation.

**This is the skill people skip, and skipping it is why projects go generic.** Agreeable discovery produces a plausible brief. A plausible brief produces plausible design. Plausible design is the statistical centre.

---

## Method

Adversarial, not hostile. The goal is a brief that survives contact with reality.

**1. Attack the goal.**
- Why this, now?
- What happens if it does not get built?
- If it succeeds completely, what changes? Name something observable.
- Is the stated goal the real goal? (Portfolios are usually about getting a specific opportunity, not "showing work".)

**2. Attack the audience.**
- Name one real person in the audience. What do they do in the 60 seconds before arriving, and the 60 after?
- What do they currently do instead?
- What would make them leave immediately?
- If the audience is "everyone", there is no audience.

**3. Attack the scope.**
- Which single piece delivers most of the value?
- If half the time vanished, what gets cut? (The answer reveals actual priority, which is rarely the stated priority.)
- Which part are you including because it is expected rather than needed?
- What is here because it is fun to build?

**4. Attack the differentiation.**
- Name three things that already exist doing roughly this. What does this do differently?
- If a competitor swapped in their logo and copy, would it still work? If yes, there is no direction yet. (The swap test, [DESIGN-TASTE.md](../DESIGN-TASTE.md) section 8.)
- What will someone remember a day later?

**5. Attack the success criteria.**
- How would you know this failed?
- Who decides, and when?
- Is any criterion unfalsifiable? Rewrite it.

**6. Attack the assumptions.** Take each logged assumption: what if the opposite is true? Which would be expensive to discover late? Those get promoted to questions.

**7. Find the contradictions.** Most briefs contain at least one — "minimal but information-dense", "for everyone but especially studios", "quick but portfolio-quality". Name them explicitly and force a resolution. An unresolved contradiction does not go away; it gets resolved implicitly at S4 by whoever is building, usually in the cheapest direction.

---

## Rules

**Follow each branch to the end** before moving to the next. Grilling that touches seven topics shallowly achieves nothing.

**Accept a good answer and move on.** This is not an endurance test. When a question is genuinely answered, stop pushing on it.

**"I don't know" is progress** — it becomes an open question with a needed-by stage.

**Do not propose solutions.** Grilling interrogates the problem. Suggesting an answer lets the user agree with you instead of thinking, and the agreement is worthless.

**Push exactly twice on vagueness.** If "premium" survives two clarification attempts, log it as unresolved and flag it as a risk to the design direction. Endless pushing on one word is its own failure.

---

## Done when

- Every non-goal is one you could defend to someone who wanted it included.
- Every success criterion could plausibly be judged unmet.
- Every contradiction is resolved or explicitly accepted with a decision recorded.
- The differentiation survives the swap test.
- You know which single piece matters most.

---

## Failure modes

| Failure | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Grilling becomes an interrogation | Accept good answers; two pushes maximum per point |
| The user gives up and says "you decide" | Offer two concrete options with consequences, not an open question |
| Contradictions get papered over | Name them literally, force a choice |
| Grilling produces anxiety, not clarity | End with a summary of what is now settled |
| Skipping it because discovery felt fine | Discovery *always* feels fine. That is the problem it exists to catch. |
