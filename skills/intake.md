# SKILL: intake

**Trigger** — a new project starts. S1.
**Owner** — Strategist
**Output** — [`PROJECT.md`](../templates/PROJECT.md)

Two passes that must stay separate: **collect**, then **challenge**. Doing both at once produces defensive answers.

**This is the highest-leverage stage in the system.** Vague scope produces generic design — a model with no constraint returns the average of everything it has seen.

---

## Pass 1 — Collect

**1. Restate the request in one sentence.** Show the restatement. Misunderstandings surface here at zero cost.

**2. Ask the mode's questions** ([modes/](../modes/)). Maximum five, batched, each with a proposed default.

**3. Separate goal from scope.** The goal is the outcome; the scope is what gets built. "Get three studio replies" is a goal; "a five-page site" is a scope. Confusing them produces projects that ship and achieve nothing.

**4. Force the non-goals.** Ask directly: *what is this deliberately not?* Minimum three. Non-goals do more work than goals — they are what stops scope inflation at S4.

**5. Make success falsifiable.** "It looks good" cannot fail. "A studio would show it in a review without apologising" can. Push until a criterion could plausibly be judged unmet.

**6. Surface constraints.** Deadline, budget, existing assets, brand rules, who else approves. Constraints found at S1 are design inputs; the same constraints at S4 are rework.

**7. Ask what assets exist.** Logo, photography, copy. The most common silent blocker, and it is resolved at S3 or not at all.

---

## Pass 2 — Challenge

Adversarial, not hostile. The goal is a brief that survives contact with reality.

**Skip this pass for game/experiment mode** — being wrong there costs an afternoon, which is an acceptable price for moving fast. **Mandatory everywhere else.**

The first answers are always the plausible ones. Agreeable discovery produces a plausible brief, a plausible brief produces plausible design, and plausible design is the statistical centre.

**Attack the goal.** Why this, now? What happens if it is not built? If it succeeds completely, what changes — name something observable. Is the stated goal the real goal? (Portfolios are usually about a specific opportunity, not "showing work".)

**Attack the audience.** Name one real person. What do they do in the 60 seconds before arriving, and after? What would make them leave immediately? **If the audience is "everyone", there is no audience.**

**Attack the scope.** Which single piece delivers most of the value? If half the time vanished, what gets cut — that answer reveals actual priority, which is rarely the stated priority. What is here because it is expected? What is here because it is fun to build?

**Attack the differentiation.** Name three things that already exist doing roughly this. What does this do differently? Would it survive the swap test ([DESIGN-TASTE.md](../DESIGN-TASTE.md) section 6)? What will someone remember a day later?

**Attack the success criteria.** How would you know this failed? Who decides, and when?

**Attack the assumptions.** For each logged assumption: what if the opposite is true? Which would be expensive to discover late? Those become questions.

**Find the contradictions.** Most briefs contain at least one — "minimal but information-dense", "for everyone but especially studios", "quick but portfolio-quality". Name them and force a resolution. **An unresolved contradiction gets resolved implicitly at S4 by whoever is building, usually in the cheapest direction.**

---

## Rules for pass 2

- **Follow each branch to the end** before moving on. Touching seven topics shallowly achieves nothing.
- **Accept a good answer and move on.** This is not an endurance test.
- **"I don't know" is progress** — it becomes an open question with a needed-by stage.
- **Do not propose solutions.** Suggesting an answer lets the user agree with you instead of thinking, and the agreement is worthless.
- **Push exactly twice on vagueness.** If "premium" survives two attempts, log it as unresolved and flag it as a risk to the direction.

---

## Done when

You can answer, without hedging:

- What is this?
- Who is it for?
- What is deliberately not in it?
- How will we know it worked?
- What do we not know yet, and when do we need to know it?

Plus: every non-goal is one you could defend to someone who wanted it included · every contradiction is resolved or explicitly accepted · the differentiation survives the swap test.

## Failure modes

| Failure | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Twelve questions, exhausted user | Five maximum, batched, with defaults |
| Accepting "make it look premium" | Ask premium *like what*, and to whom |
| Skipping non-goals on a small project | Small projects inflate fastest |
| Success criteria that cannot fail | Rewrite until they could be judged unmet |
| Discovering an asset gap at S4 | Step 7, every time |
| Designing during intake | Note the idea, move on. Direction is S3. |
| Skipping pass 2 because collection felt fine | **It always feels fine. That is what pass 2 exists to catch.** |
| Grilling becoming an interrogation | Two pushes maximum per point; end with what is now settled |
