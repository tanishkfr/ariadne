# SKILL: reference-analysis

**Trigger** — references, screenshots, or a moodboard are supplied, or a direction is needed and references must be chosen.
**Owner** — Design director
**Inputs** — reference URLs or images, `PROJECT.md`
**Output** — the mechanism list and the "What NOT to copy" section of [`DESIGN.md`](../templates/DESIGN.md)

**The core distinction: mechanisms transfer, surfaces do not.** Copying a surface produces a knock-off. Extracting a mechanism produces a direction. Synthesising the results into a direction: [DESIGN-TASTE.md](../DESIGN-TASTE.md) section 7.

**Evidence boundary** — found, inspected, and used are different states. Mark a
reference inspected only when a dated retrieval, visual capture, or provider
transcript exists and is hashed in the project creative-evidence ledger. An
inaccessible source has a blocker and no observations. Mark this skill used only
when a traced DESIGN.md decision cites an inspected source.

---

## Method

**1. Require at least three references.** One produces imitation. Two produces a blend. Three force synthesis, because three sources cannot all be satisfied at once and the resolution is where originality comes from.

If only one is supplied, ask for two more or propose two from [references/visual-references.md](../references/visual-references.md) and say which you chose and why.

**2. Look properly.** Load the site. Scroll it. Resize it. Use it on a phone. Watch what happens on interaction. A screenshot shows composition; it hides motion, pacing, and behaviour, which is usually where the quality lives.

**3. For each reference, answer six questions.**

- What is the **organising principle**? (a grid, a sequence, a material, a metaphor, a constraint)
- What does the **type** do that a default would not?
- What is the **colour strategy**, and where did it come from?
- What does **motion** do structurally — reveal, pace, orient, characterise?
- What is **deliberately absent**?
- **Why does it feel expensive?** Usually restraint, extreme type contrast, real photography, or motion that responds precisely.

**4. Rewrite every answer as a mechanism.**

- Surface: "black background with big white serif type"
- Mechanism: "a single achromatic field so the only variable is type scale, which makes size alone carry hierarchy"

Test: could this be applied to a completely different subject? If not, it is still a surface.

**5. Pool the mechanisms** from all references into one list. Drop the attribution — which site each came from stops mattering the moment you are synthesising.

**6. Name the tension.** Most memorable work holds two opposed qualities: rigorous but playful, archival but immediate, brutal but warm. Naming the tension you want resolves dozens of downstream decisions on its own.

**7. Write the "What NOT to copy" list.** Mandatory, minimum three, specific. Not "don't copy the layout" — "do not use their split-screen scroll transition; it is the thing people recognise their site by."

---

## Constraints

- **No single reference may contribute more than two mechanisms.** Three or more means you are rebuilding that site.
- **Never lift layout structure.** Mechanisms transfer; page structure is the surface.
- **Change the domain.** If the reference is a photography portfolio and this is a boxing game, the mechanism must survive translation. If it cannot, it was a surface.

---

## Handling screenshots of existing products

Treat as evidence, not instruction. A screenshot showing a pattern is not approval to reuse it. Run every observed pattern through the anti-generic table ([DESIGN-TASTE.md](../DESIGN-TASTE.md) section 6) before adopting it — the most-screenshotted patterns are frequently the most generic ones.

---

## Done when

- Three or more references analysed.
- Mechanisms pooled and de-attributed.
- The tension is named.
- The "What NOT to copy" list has at least three specific entries.
- No reference contributed more than two mechanisms.

---

## Failure modes

| Failure | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Describing surfaces and calling them mechanisms | Apply the different-subject test to each |
| One reference dominating | Two-mechanism cap, counted explicitly |
| Analysing screenshots instead of the live site | Load it; motion and pacing are invisible in stills |
| Skipping the "do not copy" list | Mandatory section in `DESIGN.md`; G1 checks it |
| References chosen because they are famous | Choose for mechanism relevance to *this* subject |
